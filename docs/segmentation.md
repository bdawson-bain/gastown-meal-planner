# Target Segment: Fitness-Focused Singles

## Decision

The product targets **fitness-focused singles**: adults living alone who treat nutrition as an active part of their fitness or health practice, not just a background necessity.

---

## Who They Are

- Age 22–40, living alone or with roommates but cooking independently
- Track macros, follow training plans, or maintain a structured diet (cutting, bulking, maintenance)
- Frequent gym-goers, recreational athletes, or health-optimizers
- Comfortable with apps; already use fitness trackers, calorie counters, or workout logging tools
- Time-constrained on weekdays but willing to batch-cook on weekends

---

## Why They Fit This Product

**1. High engagement motivation.** For this segment, food is part of the fitness system — not an afterthought. They have strong intrinsic motivation to plan meals carefully, which drives the active, repeated engagement meal planning apps depend on.

**2. Structured needs that map to core features.** They need protein targets hit, calorie totals tracked, and shopping lists that match a weekly training cycle. These are concrete, recurring jobs-to-be-done that meal planning software solves directly.

**3. Single-person household economics.** No one else's preferences to negotiate. They can adopt a plan and stick to it without coordinating around kids, partners, or dietary opposites. Adoption friction is lower; plan adherence is higher.

**4. Willingness to pay.** This segment spends on gym memberships, supplements, and workout gear. Meal planning is a natural adjacent spend — the value framing ("optimize your nutrition") fits their existing mental model.

**5. Word-of-mouth density.** Fitness communities (gym friends, online fitness groups, running clubs) are tightly networked and share tool recommendations. A product that lands well here spreads laterally within the segment.

---

## Tradeoffs vs. Other Candidate Segments

### Budget-conscious families

**Why they were considered:** Large households generate significant per-plan value; parents are motivated by feeding multiple people efficiently.

**Why we deprioritized them:** Higher coordination complexity per user (multiple dietary needs, picky eaters, school schedules). Willingness to pay is more price-sensitive. The product's current feature set doesn't have the family-coordination layer (shared shopping, role-based access, kid-friendly filtering) that would make it compelling vs. paper lists or simple grocery apps.

### Time-starved professionals

**Why they were considered:** Strong motivation to reduce decision fatigue around food; income to support a paid product.

**Why we deprioritized them:** Their core need is speed and zero-effort — they want to order in or heat a meal kit, not plan. Meal planning requires up-front time investment that conflicts with the "I have no time" core complaint. Retention risk is high: the first stressful week leads to abandonment. Fitness-focused singles already accept the time cost of food prep as part of their routine.

---

## Summary

Fitness-focused singles have the motivation, habits, and existing tooling patterns that make meal planning software a natural fit. The segment is reachable, willing to engage deeply, and willing to pay. The tradeoffs against families and professionals are real but reflect product-stage constraints: this segment requires fewer specialized features to deliver value and is more likely to stick with the product through early iterations.
